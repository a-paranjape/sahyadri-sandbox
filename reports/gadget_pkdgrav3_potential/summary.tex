\begin{abstract}
\noindent \gad{} and \pkd{} were run from identical initial conditions (\gad{} NGenIC, Sahyadri default settings,
200\,\Mpch) with $32^3$ and $256^3$ particles. Each run went through the full Sahyadri halo pipeline (Rockstar,
consistent-trees, VAHC), and the two codes are compared particle by particle (potential, position, velocity), halo by
halo, and through the stacked potential around halos in mass bins out to 50\,\Mpch. At $256^3$:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Particles.} At $z=12$ the potentials agree to $6\times10^{-3}$ rms (correlation $1-6\times10^{-6}$). The
difference grows to 2\% at $z=3$ and 22\% at $z=0$, and 75--90\% of it comes from the particles ending up in
different places: at $z=0$ the median separation is 0.12\,\Mpch{} and the median velocity difference 70\,km\,s$^{-1}$,
dominated by halo particles. Once \pkd{}'s softened self-potential is removed, the zero points agree to
$\le1.2\times10^3$\,\kms.
\item \textbf{Halos.} Mass functions agree within 2--3\% above $10^{12}$\,\Msunh. Matched halos have the same masses,
spins and VAHC bias, but \pkd{} halos are 20--25\% more concentrated at $10^{12}$--$10^{13}$\,\Msunh, with 2--5\%
higher $V_{\max}$ and 4--9\% deeper $\phi_{\min}$. This is consistent with \pkd{}'s smaller effective softening
under the production settings. For the same particles, the mean potentials of matched halos agree to 0.5\%.
\item \textbf{Stacked potential.} Around halos the stacked mean potential agrees to 1--2\% ($\le0.02\,\sigma_\phi$)
from 0.2 to 50\,\Mpch{} at $z = 3$, 1, 0.5 and 0. Inside 0.1\,\Mpch{} \pkd{} is 3--6\% deeper.
The volume-weighted mean (potential on a $512^3$ grid) is much shallower than the particle-weighted one and tends to
zero far from the halos. Its code difference is within 2 paired errors ($\le0.016\,\sigma_\phi$) at every radius
from 0.4 to 50\,\Mpch, in both 36 log bins and 250 linear bins of 0.2\,\Mpch.
\end{itemize}
The stacking tool keeps only per-mass-bin sums and dilutes the outer shells by particle ID. A stack of a $256^3$
snapshot with $\sim17\,000$ halos takes $\sim10$\,s; gridding the potential for the volume weighting takes 6\,min.
\end{abstract}
